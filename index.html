Our Story

Align exists because I watched too many people pay $200 a month for a studio membership they used twice — and then blame themselves.

After eight years teaching mat and reformer Pilates, I noticed the pattern: the people who transformed weren't the most motivated or the most flexible. They were the ones with a plan they didn't have to think about. A structure that decided for them.

So I spent eighteen months distilling everything from the studio into a system anyone could run at home — the exact progressions, the cueing, the pacing — and priced it at less than a single private session. No subscription, because your fitness shouldn't have a meter running on it.

Fifty thousand downloads later, the principle hasn't changed: precision over intensity, consistency over heroics. I hope you'll join us.

Before You Ask

Everything you're wondering

Do I need any equipment?
No. Every session in the home track is built around your body weight. A mat is nice to have; a light resistance ring is optional for certain progressions — and every session works without it.
I'm a complete beginner — will I actually be able to follow it?
Yes. The Beginner track assumes zero experience. Every movement is broken down step by step with photos of correct positioning, common mistakes to avoid, and modifications built into every session.
How much time do I need?
Most sessions run 25–35 minutes, five days a week, with two rest days. The program is deliberately built to fit inside a lunch break or a nap time.
Is this a subscription?
No — and it never becomes one. One payment of $47 buys lifetime access, and every future update is included free. You will never be billed again.
How do I get the files after purchase?
Instantly. After checkout you're taken straight to your download page, and a backup link is emailed to you within minutes. Everything is yours to keep offline, forever.
What devices does it work on?
Everything. The digital editions are designed mobile-first for the phone propped against your water bottle, and the PDFs print beautifully in A4 or US Letter.
I have an injury / I'm pregnant — is this safe for me?
The program includes modifications for common limitations (wrists, knees, lower back) and notes where movements should be adapted or skipped during pregnancy. That said, always check with your doctor or physiotherapist before starting any new program — they know your situation; we don't.
