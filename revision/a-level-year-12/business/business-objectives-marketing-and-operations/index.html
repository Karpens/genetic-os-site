What is business and management decision making

Key points

  • A business adds value by converting inputs into outputs customers will pay more for; mission statements express purpose and values.
  • Corporate objectives cascade into functional objectives for marketing, operations, finance and human resources; SMART objectives are specific, measurable, achievable, realistic and time-bound.
  • Stakeholder and shareholder approaches differ in whose interests take priority.
  • Decision making may be scientific (data-driven, using decision trees and forecasting) or based on intuition; risk and uncertainty affect both.
  • Leadership styles include autocratic, paternalistic, democratic and laissez-faire; the best style depends on context, task and team.
  • Opportunity cost and trade-offs feature in every significant business decision.

In more detail

  • Objective cascade example: corporate objective "grow revenue by 15% in 3 years" → marketing launches two new products, operations raises capacity by 20%, HR recruits 30 staff, and finance raises £2m.
  • Worked opportunity cost: a firm with £100,000 can buy a machine returning £12,000 a year or run a campaign returning £9,000. Choosing the machine has an opportunity cost of £9,000 a year.
  • Scientific vs intuitive: a supermarket using loyalty-card data to plan store layouts is scientific, while a founder backing a gut feeling about a new trend is intuitive. Both carry risk.
  • A-level skill: link each decision to its effect on the four functions and the firm's objectives, then judge how far it is likely to succeed.

What is business and management decision making: 7 questions

Pick an answer to check it, or open the answer for the explanation.

1. What does SMART stand for in objectives?

  1. Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, Time-bound
  2. Simple, Modern, Accurate, Rapid, Tested
  3. Strategic, Market, Asset, Revenue, Target
  4. Sales, Money, Assets, Risk, Timing
Show answer

A: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, Time-bound. SMART objectives are easier to monitor and evaluate.

2. What is a mission statement?

  1. A financial forecast
  2. A statement of a business's purpose and values
  3. A marketing campaign
  4. A legal document
Show answer

B: A statement of a business's purpose and values. It guides objectives and decisions.

3. Which leadership style involves delegating decisions to the team with minimal direction?

  1. Autocratic
  2. Paternalistic
  3. Democratic
  4. Laissez-faire
Show answer

D: Laissez-faire. It works best with skilled, motivated teams.

4. What is the shareholder approach?

  1. Prioritising all stakeholder interests equally
  2. Prioritising returns to the owners of the business
  3. Ignoring profit
  4. Focusing only on employees
Show answer

B: Prioritising returns to the owners of the business. The stakeholder approach balances wider interests.

5. What is a decision tree used for?

  1. Organising staff
  2. Comparing options using probabilities and expected values
  3. Setting wages
  4. Advertising products
Show answer

B: Comparing options using probabilities and expected values. It supports scientific decision making under risk.

6. Which internal factor might most influence a firm's choice between an autocratic and democratic leadership style?

  1. The weather
  2. The skills and motivation of the workforce
  3. The exchange rate
  4. Foreign competition
Show answer

B: The skills and motivation of the workforce. A skilled, motivated team often responds better to a more democratic style.

7. What is a limitation of using only quantitative sales forecasts for decision making?

  1. They are always accurate
  2. They may not capture qualitative factors such as changing consumer tastes
  3. They take too little data into account
  4. They cannot be calculated
Show answer

B: They may not capture qualitative factors such as changing consumer tastes. Numbers alone may miss important contextual changes.
