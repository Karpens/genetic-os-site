Circular motion: horizontal and vertical circles

Key points

  • For motion in a circle of radius r with angular speed ω: v = rω, and the acceleration towards the centre is v²/r = rω².
  • Newton's second law towards the centre: F = mv²/r = mrω², where F is the resultant of forces such as tension, friction, normal reaction or a component of weight.
  • Horizontal circles: conical pendulum (T cos θ = mg, T sin θ = mrω²); car on a banked track; particle on a rough turntable (friction provides the force).
  • Vertical circles: speed changes; use conservation of energy for the speed at each point and F = mv²/r for tension or reaction. A particle on a string completes the circle if the tension at the top is ≥ 0 (v_top² ≥ gr); on a rigid rod it only needs v_top ≥ 0.

In more detail

  • Worked example: a 0.5 kg ball on a 1 m string has speed 6 m/s at the bottom (g = 9.8). At the top: v² = 36 − 4g = 36 − 39.2 < 0, so it cannot reach the top; it leaves the circle before then.
  • Banking without friction: tan θ = v²/(rg), the speed at which no sideways friction is needed.
  • Exam tip: resolve towards the centre for the circular equation, and vertically (or along the radius) for the other.

Circular motion: horizontal and vertical circles: 10 questions

Pick an answer to check it, or open the answer for the explanation.

1. What is the acceleration of a particle moving in a circle of radius 2 m at 6 m/s?

  1. 3 m/s²
  2. 18 m/s² towards the centre
  3. 12 m/s²
  4. 72 m/s²
Show answer

B: 18 m/s² towards the centre. v²/r = 36/2.

2. Convert angular speed: v = 10 m/s, r = 5 m. What is ω?

  1. 50 rad/s
  2. 0.5 rad/s
  3. 15 rad/s
  4. 2 rad/s
Show answer

D: 2 rad/s. ω = v/r.

3. What provides the centripetal force for a car turning on a flat road?

  1. Weight
  2. Air resistance
  3. Friction
  4. Normal reaction alone
Show answer

C: Friction. It acts towards the centre.

4. In a conical pendulum, which equation is correct vertically?

  1. T cos θ = mg
  2. T sin θ = mg
  3. T = mg
  4. T = mrω²
Show answer

A: T cos θ = mg. No vertical acceleration.

5. For a particle on a string in a vertical circle, what is the condition to complete the circle?

  1. Speed at the top > 0
  2. Tension at the top ≥ 0
  3. Speed at the bottom = 0
  4. Tension at the bottom = 0
Show answer

B: Tension at the top ≥ 0. Otherwise the string goes slack.

6. What is the minimum speed at the top of a vertical circle of radius r on a string?

  1. 0
  2. √(2gr)
  3. gr
  4. √(gr)
Show answer

D: √(gr). mg = mv²/r when tension is zero.

7. A 2 kg mass moves in a horizontal circle of radius 0.5 m at ω = 4 rad/s. What is the resultant force?

  1. 8 N
  2. 4 N
  3. 16 N
  4. 32 N
Show answer

C: 16 N. mrω² = 2 × 0.5 × 16.

8. Why use energy in vertical circles?

  1. The speed changes with height
  2. The speed is constant
  3. There is no gravity
  4. Momentum is conserved
Show answer

A: The speed changes with height. KE converts to GPE.

9. A particle on a rigid rod moves in a vertical circle. What is needed to complete the circle?

  1. Tension at the top ≥ 0
  2. Speed at the top > 0 (just reaching it)
  3. v_top² ≥ gr
  4. Nothing
Show answer

B: Speed at the top > 0 (just reaching it). A rod can push as well as pull.

10. On a banked track with no friction, what provides the centripetal force?

  1. Weight
  2. Friction
  3. Air resistance
  4. The horizontal component of the normal reaction
Show answer

D: The horizontal component of the normal reaction. R sin θ = mv²/r.
